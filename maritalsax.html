Turning a genuinely unique performance duo into a digital experience that earns trust and converts bookings — fast.

MaritalSax is Talon and Len'I McKinney — a husband-and-wife saxophone duo who bill themselves as the world's first of their kind, with 52+ years of combined experience across 1,000+ events across Chicagoland.

Their distinctiveness was both the opportunity and the challenge. Couples and corporate planners book on emotional confidence and fast proof of quality. My job was to design a site that earns that trust in seconds, converts visitors into consultations, and surfaces the duo in local search.

The Problem

Four design problems to solve

Event entertainment is a crowded, trust-driven market, and most competitors look the same — generic stock imagery, a contact form, and a list of services. A booker scanning a dozen vendor sites makes a gut decision fast.

01
Communicate a unique identity instantly
A husband-and-wife sax duo is a genuine differentiator — but only if the site makes that emotional hook land in the first screen, not three scrolls down.
02
Build trust faster than a competitor can
Bookers have been burned before. The site needed to front-load proof: real performance footage, social proof, and concrete experience numbers.
03
Reduce friction from interest to booking
The path from "this looks great" to "I've started a booking" had to be obvious and low-effort, with clear expectations at every step.
04
Get found locally
None of the above matters if planners searching for a "Chicago saxophone duo" or "wedding saxophonist" never reach the page in the first place.

My Role

End-to-end ownership

This was a full-scope project — not a handoff. Every decision, from brand to search visibility, ran through one designer.

End-to-end web design — every page designed from a blank canvas, including layout, hierarchy, and component design.
Custom UI & CSS — extensive custom CSS and bespoke color work on top of WordPress; off-the-shelf components were redesigned, not reused.
Brand identity — designed the primary logo and a secondary animated brand mark / watermark used as a motion signature across the experience.
Hero video — compiled and edited the hero performance reel myself, pacing footage to land the emotional payoff within seconds of landing.
Interactive performance map — built a custom Leaflet map plotting 22+ Chicagoland cities; a credibility device and a local-SEO signal simultaneously.
Conversion design — structured the booking journey and CTA hierarchy around a clear three-step flow with friction removed at every point.
SEO & content — rewrote all on-page copy for local search intent and added descriptive, keyword-aware metadata to every image, video, and logo on the site.

Process

Thinking before designing

"Couples buy emotion and a story; planners buy reliability and a stress-free process. The page needed to serve both without diluting either."

The two audiences — engaged couples and corporate/event planners — buy for different reasons. That insight shaped the page's rhythm: emotional, sensory sections alternating with reassurance sections.

1
Understanding the brand and the buyer
I mapped both audience types — couples and corporate planners — against their buying motivations. Couples buy emotion and story; planners buy reliability and a stress-free handoff. Every section was written for both without compromising either.
2
Information architecture
I sequenced the homepage as a narrative funnel rather than a brochure: Hook → Differentiate → Reassure → Prove → Act. Emotional and sensory sections alternate with trust and proof sections, keeping both audience types engaged through the scroll.
3
Visual direction
The aesthetic target was "upscale live music" — elegant, warm, and confident without being stuffy. I built a custom color palette and applied it through hand-written CSS, so type, spacing, and color felt intentional and cohesive across every section rather than templated.
4
Working within (and against) WordPress
WordPress was the delivery platform — a real-world constraint. A meaningful part of the craft was redesigning default components and writing custom CSS to make the site feel bespoke rather than off-the-shelf, exactly the kind of pragmatic problem-solving production work demands.

Key Design Decisions

Decisions that moved the needle

Hero
Video as the emotional hook
I led with a performance reel I edited myself rather than a static image. Live music is a sensory product — a hero video lets a visitor hear and feel the chemistry in seconds, which a photograph and headline never could. The edit was paced deliberately to land that emotional payoff fast, paired with positioning copy that names the differentiator outright.
Brand
A brand identity built for motion
I designed the primary logo and a secondary animated brand mark / watermark. Having a motion version of the identity meant the brand could live consistently across video content and the site as a recurring signature, reinforcing recognition rather than appearing only once in the header.
UX Feature
The interactive Chicagoland performance map
Rather than claiming the duo plays all over Chicago, I built an interactive Leaflet map plotting 22+ real performance locations — from the Gold Coast and Hyde Park to Naperville and Lake Forest. It converts an abstract credibility claim into something concrete and explorable. For a planner deciding whether the duo covers their venue's area, it answers the question interactively and doubles as social proof. It also surfaces real place names as on-page content for local SEO.
Trust
Layered trust architecture
Credibility is distributed throughout the page rather than dumped in one block: concrete framing (52+ years combined, 1,000+ events), testimonials at multiple scroll points, a full list of signature performance types so a visitor immediately sees their event covered, and "team player" language aimed at planners who need reliability above all else.
Conversion
A frictionless, transparent booking flow
The booking journey is presented as a simple, demystified three-step process — Get a Quote → Book with a Deposit → Plan Your Event — with clear expectations at each stage, including the 20% deposit and what planning support looks like. Reducing uncertainty in the process is itself a conversion lever: people commit more easily when they can see the whole path.
SEO
Search visibility as a design responsibility
I rewrote every block of copy to match how real bookers actually search — leading with phrases like "Chicago saxophone duo," "wedding saxophonist," and specific event-type and neighborhood language. Every image, video, and logo was given descriptive, keyword-aware alt text, captions, and titles. The interactive map also feeds search engines a dense, authentic set of local geographic signals by surfacing real Chicago-area place names as on-page content.

Reflection

What this project taught me

⚙️
Real constraints are a portfolio strength
Designing for WordPress — rather than a blank Figma-to-code pipeline — shows you can ship bespoke quality inside the systems clients actually use. That's production design.
🎬
Motion earns the first impression
In a sensory market, leading with an edited video instead of a static hero was the highest-leverage decision on the page. Live music has to be felt to be sold.
📍
Specificity builds trust
The performance map turned a generic claim into something concrete and explorable. Showing beats telling — one feature doing double duty as a UX device and an SEO signal.
