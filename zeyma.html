Not an “African-looking scarf.” A contemporary fashion product that happens to carry a strong Ghanaian identity.

Zeyma is an accessories brand for young Ghanaians and the diaspora, starting with the scarf. The question behind it: how do you translate Ghanaian identity into a visual language that an 18-year-old would be proud to wear in Accra, Kumasi, London, New York or Berlin?

I designed the brand idea, an Adinkra-inspired pattern system, colour palettes, composition rules and the collection structure — built as a reusable design system rather than a one-off print.

The Challenge

Heritage that doesn’t read as a souvenir

Adinkra is rich, meaningful and instantly recognisable — which is exactly the trap. Repeat a symbol across a rectangle and you get a souvenir. The brief was to make something a young person would reach for on a normal Tuesday and still feel something about.

“Accra meets global street fashion” — not “traditional Ghanaian textile printed on a scarf.”

01
Fashion first
Modern composition, typography, spacing and colour lead. Culture is the voice inside the design, not a costume over it.
02
Wearable everywhere
With jeans, sneakers, a hoodie, a suit, a dress or traditional dress — it has to belong in the wardrobe, not just at festivals.
03
Ghanaian, globally fluent
One strong cultural idea plus excellent graphic design — legible to someone in Berlin, meaningful to someone in Kumasi.

Visual Language

Adinkra as a language, not a stamp

“Symbol → repeat → symbol → repeat” is decoration. Instead I studied the geometry and the philosophy behind each symbol and chose a specific design move for it: simplify, enlarge, rotate, crop, overlap, or turn it into a pattern.

Pattern System

Five layers, one brand-specific system

Rather than downloading symbols and tiling them, I built a layered system inspired by Adinkra — so every scarf in the range is recognisably Zeyma.

Layer 1
Primary symbol
A single large Sankofa form that anchors the composition.
Layer 2
Secondary symbols
Small Gye Nyame and Dwennimmen marks that reward a closer look.
Layer 3
Geometry
Lines, circles, grids and diamonds drawn from Ghanaian geometric structures — the connective tissue between symbols.
Layer 4
Texture
Subtle surface cues from Kente weaving, woven cloth, hand stamping, wood carving, brass and traditional printing.
Layer 5
Typography
Very small words and phrases — never dominant.
GHANA · ROOTS · SANKOFA · ADINKRA · ACCRA · KUMASI · COAST · ORIGIN
Signature motif. On top of existing symbols, I’d create a new graphic motif of Zeyma’s own — a blend of the logo, a Sankofa-inspired curve and Kente geometry — so the identity can travel to shirts, jackets, bags, caps, packaging, labels and social, and a scarf can be recognised without seeing the logo.

Beyond Adinkra

Ghanaian identity is bigger than one system

Kente
Not copying a cloth — studying stripes, colour relationships, weaving, repetition, symmetry and proportion, and extracting a colour rhythm.
Architecture
Old Accra, coastal forts, compounds, windows, doors and decorative patterns — subtle geometric elements.
Landscape
Atlantic Ocean, red earth, tropical vegetation and Accra streets as collection themes.
Signage & type
Tro-tro lettering, shop signs, hand-painted ads, taxis, markets, football culture, street posters — their visual character, not a copy.

Composition & Product

Designing the scarf as a product, not a rectangle

Zones

The ends are the branding opportunity. A distinctive border that appears on every scarf in the collection builds recognition — “that’s a Zeyma scarf” — before anyone reads a logo.

Main patternAdinkra + geometric composition
Brand borderThe same on every scarf
Adinkra motifEnds and fringe designed separately

A loud side and a quiet side

Not both faces need to be equally busy. Two styling personalities in one product is far more commercially useful.

Side A
Loud → cultural statement

Bold Adinkra pattern.

Side B
Subtle → everyday fashion

Mostly black or cream with one small repeating motif.

Asymmetry
Traditional patterns lean on symmetry; youth fashion doesn’t have to. A large cropped Sankofa on one side, small geometry, text and empty space on the other gives an editorial feel.
Negative space
At least 20–30% breathing room. Fill every centimetre and it becomes textile wallpaper; leave room and it feels premium.
Fabric
Satin for vibrance, cotton for everyday, silk for premium — and woven jacquard, where the Adinkra is woven into the cloth instead of printed.

Language & Care

Words need to be right

Ghanaian-language words make a design feel far more authentic than “GHANA” alone — but only if they’re correct.

SANKOFAAKWAABAYƐN KOAYANYAMEADWUMA
Before production. Every word, spelling and meaning is verified with a reliable Ghanaian source or native speaker. A beautiful scarf with a linguistic mistake is not a risk worth taking.
